\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
% Paragraph 1: setting and motivation.
% ------------------------------------------------------------------
Prepare-and-measure protocols certify secrecy from the structure of the
observed statistics: Alice prepares one of a set of states labeled
$x$, Bob measures with a setting $y$ and obtains an outcome $b$, and the
pair distills a shared secret from the correlations $P(b|x,y)$ together
with the operational equivalences that the preparations and measurements
are promised to satisfy. When the statistics witness preparation
contextuality~\cite{Spekkens2005}, no eavesdropper whose side information
is mediated by a noncontextual (classical) model can predict the
outcomes perfectly, and a positive secret-key rate can survive.

literature.}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
% Paragraph 2: the security paradigm considered in this work.
% ------------------------------------------------------------------
In this work we analyze security against \emph{individual
entangling-probe attacks with delayed, setting-dependent product
measurements}. In each round, the eavesdropper Eve attaches an ancillary
probe to the system in transit and lets it interact, but she does not
measure immediately: she waits until the classical announcement of the
measurement setting $y$ (and, where relevant, of which rounds are
key-generating), and only then measures her probe, with a measurement
that may depend arbitrarily on $y$. Crucially, the attack is
\emph{individual}: Eve's probe interacts with each round separately and
is measured with a product (round-by-round) strategy, rather than a
collective or coherent attack across rounds. Within this paradigm we
consider two classes of adversary, matched to the two solver backends of
our accompanying code. The \emph{operational adversary} is constrained
only by the operational theory reproducing the observed statistics: her
side information is an arbitrary decomposition of the observed behavior
into subnormalized behaviors consistent with the promised operational
equivalences. Bounding this adversary's guessing probability is a linear
program (LP). The \emph{quantum adversary} is additionally constrained
by quantum theory: the joint state of message and probe, and Eve's
setting-dependent probe measurements, must admit a quantum realization.
Bounding this adversary is a noncommutative polynomial optimization
problem, which we relax to a semidefinite program (SDP) in the spirit of
the Navascu\'es--Pironio--Ac\'in hierarchy~\cite{NPA2008}, handling
nonprojective measurements through an explicit Naimark-unitary
construction. \todo{Sharpen the formal definitions and point to the
technical sections.}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
% Paragraph 3: from protocols to key rates (the pipeline).
% ------------------------------------------------------------------
Our second contribution is a general-purpose, open-source pipeline that
converts a protocol specification into certified key rates. A protocol
is specified as a contextuality scenario: a behavior $P(b|x,y)$ (given
numerically, or generated from quantum states and effects, or from
states and effects of a generalized probabilistic theory) together with
its preparation and measurement operational equivalences, which the code
can also discover automatically. From this data the pipeline computes,
for each measurement setting $y$, Eve's optimal guessing probability
$\pguess(\cdot\,|y)$ for the raw key variable---held by Alice or by Bob,
as configured---under either adversary model, and converts these
guessing probabilities into uncertainties (min-entropy, or the tighter
reverse-Fano bound) and thence into asymptotic secret-key rates by
subtracting the error-correction cost between the honest parties.
Because every optimization is solved together with its dual, the
pipeline additionally returns human-readable \emph{witness
inequalities}: linear functionals of the observed behavior that certify
the reported bound and expose exactly which features of the statistics
power the security claim. \todo{Forward-reference the sections deriving
the LP, the SDP, and the key-rate formulas.}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
% Paragraph 4: the where_key distinction and key-rate bookkeeping.
% ------------------------------------------------------------------
A structural distinction that our framework makes explicit is
\emph{which rounds generate key}. In the simplest protocols every
preparation setting $x$ and every measurement setting $y$ is used to
establish a key dit, and the key rate per experimental run coincides
with the key rate per key-generating round. In general, however, a
protocol may designate only a subset of preparations as key-generating
for each measurement setting---in our code, a nontrivial \wherekey{}
assignment listing, for each $y$, the values of $x$ whose rounds
contribute to the key---with the remaining rounds spent on parameter
estimation or discarded at sifting. This gives the notion of ``key rate
per experimental run'' a more nuanced meaning: it is the key rate per
key-generating round discounted by the fraction of rounds that are
key-generating at all, and optimizing the choice of \wherekey{} (which
our pipeline can do automatically) trades the quality of each key dit
against the rate at which key rounds occur. \todo{Give a small worked
example contrasting trivial and nontrivial \wherekey{}.}

\subsection{Warm-up: key from a single measurement setting}
\label{sec:results-single-setting}

We begin with the simplest protocol our framework analyzes: one in which
only a \emph{single} measurement setting of Bob is ever used to establish
key. This case is pedagogically privileged. When several settings
generate key, Eve profits from holding a quantum memory: she lets her
probe interact with the system in transit, waits for the public
announcement of Bob's setting $y$, and only then measures her probe with
a $y$-dependent measurement. But when the key is distilled from a single
setting $y^{\ast}$, nothing is gained by waiting: on every
key-generating round the announcement is a foregone conclusion, and
Eve's optimal individual attack collapses to one fixed probe measurement
chosen in advance. The full apparatus of delayed, setting-dependent
product measurements --- introduced in
Sec.~\ref{sec:adversaries} --- is therefore not yet needed here,
and the reader may treat this subsection as a self-contained warm-up.
(Parameter-estimation rounds on the other settings are still essential:
they are what pins down the observed behavior, and with it Eve's
constraints.)

The protocol is a deliberate twist on BB84~\cite{BB84}. Alice prepares,
uniformly at random, one of the four qubit states
$\ket{0},\ket{1},\ket{+},\ket{-}$ --- the BB84 states, whose two basis
ensembles furnish the preparation operational equivalence
$\tfrac12\rho_{0}+\tfrac12\rho_{1}=\tfrac12\rho_{+}+\tfrac12\rho_{-}$.
Bob, however, does \emph{not} measure in the $Z$ and $X$ bases. His two
settings are the intermediate bases rotated by $\pi/4$ in the $X$--$Z$
plane (eigenbases at angles $\pi/4$ and $3\pi/4$). The raw key dit for a
round is, on Alice's side, the modal outcome of the announced
measurement for her preparation, and on Bob's side his actual outcome;
the mismatch is paid for in error correction, at cost
$H(K|B,y^{\ast})=h\!\left(\cos^{2}\tfrac{\pi}{8}\right)\approx 0.6009$
bits per key round, where $h$ is the binary entropy.

Why not plain BB84? Because against the operational adversary, plain
BB84 --- Alice's four states, Bob measuring $Z$ and $X$ --- certifies
\emph{nothing}. Our LP, which is exact for that adversary, returns
$\pguess = \tfrac34$ for either setting, and the resulting key rate
vanishes ($0.0000$ bits reverse-Fano; the min-entropy bound is strictly
negative). No qubit assumption enters our framework: Eve is free to
realize the observed BB84 behavior and its operational equivalences on a
carrier of any dimension, and in that larger arena the noiseless-BB84
intuition ``no disturbance implies no information'' simply fails. (The
same statistics \emph{do} certify key under an explicit dimension bound
\cite{PawlowskiBrunner2011}; we return to the comparison between
dimension assumptions and operational-equivalence assumptions in
Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}.) Rotating Bob's measurements away from the
preparation bases restores security: intuitively, the rotated statistics
force any operational decomposition available to Eve to spread its
weight across both of Alice's basis ensembles.

Table~\ref{tab:single-setting} collects the noiseless numbers for the
rotated protocol with key generated only at $y^{\ast}=0$. Against the
operational adversary the LP returns, exactly,
\begin{equation}
  \pguess(y^{\ast}) \;=\; 1-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}\;\approx\;0.6464,
\end{equation}
and with it a positive reverse-Fano key rate of $0.1062$ bits per
key-generating round. Because only half of all rounds are
key-generating (Bob's other setting is pure parameter estimation), the
key rate \emph{per experimental run} is half that, $0.0531$ bits --- our
first encounter with the distinction, developed in
Sec.~\ref{sec:keyrates}, that a nontrivial \wherekey{} assignment drives
a wedge between the two bookkeepings. Against the quantum adversary the
SDP certifies $\pguess(y^{\ast})\le 0.5001$ --- Eve learns essentially
nothing --- so the certified quantum key rate, $0.3989$ bits per key
round, nearly saturates the error-correction-limited maximum
$1-h(\cos^{2}\tfrac{\pi}{8})\approx 0.3991$.

The LP also hands us its dual certificate. For the key-generating
setting the (L2-refined) witness inequality reads
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:single-setting-witness}
  \pguess(y^{\ast}=0)\;\le\;\frac12
  +\frac14\Bigl[P(0|0,1)+P(1|1,1)+P(1|2,1)+P(0|3,1)\Bigr],
\end{equation}
valid for every behavior satisfying the protocol's operational
equivalences; it is tight at the observed data, where each bracketed
term equals $\sin^{2}\tfrac{\pi}{8}$. The witness exposes the security
mechanism at a glance: Eve's ability to guess the key generated at
$y=0$ is controlled entirely by how strongly the data at the
\emph{other} setting deviates from the outcome-deterministic extremes
--- the parameter-estimation setting polices the key-generating one.

Figure~\ref{fig:single-setting-noise} traces both key rates as the
preparations are depolarized with visibility $\eta$ (each state mixed
with the maximally mixed state, which preserves the preparation
operational equivalence). The operational-adversary rate stays positive
down to $\eta^{\ast}\approx 0.932$. The quantum-adversary curve is
computed at level~2 of the SDP hierarchy; being a relaxation, it is a
certified \emph{lower} bound on the true quantum key rate everywhere,
whereas the LP curve is exact for the operational adversary --- an
asymmetry we dwell on in Sec.~\ref{sec:results-porac}.
